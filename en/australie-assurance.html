Working holiday Australia

Working holiday insurance the one cost where saving money costs you.

Cover • Repatriation • Excess • Sports • Exclusions

The visa does not formally require it. Australian reality does. Here is how to compare policies without getting caught out on a single line.

In this guide

  1. Why it is non negotiable for a French citizen
  2. The six criteria to compare
  3. The classic traps
  4. What I chose
  5. Frequently asked questions

Why it is non negotiable for a French citizen

Australia has reciprocal health care agreements with eleven countries. France is not one of them. Check whether your own country is on the list.

In practice, a French passport gives no right to Medicare. No partial cover, no safety net. Whatever you do not get covered, you pay for.

And the amounts have nothing in common with French prices. A consultation, an X ray after a scooter fall, a few days in hospital: the bill climbs into thousands of Australian dollars, with no solidarity cap behind it.

French social security, for its part, does not follow you. As soon as you leave to work abroad outside Europe, you leave the system. The European health card is useless here.

The six criteria to compare

Every policy shows a monthly price. That is not where the difference lies.

The medical cost limit

It is the most important figure. Look at the total limit and check that it is counted in hundreds of thousands of euros, not tens of thousands.

Repatriation

Check that it is unlimited and that it includes repatriation of remains, an unpleasant topic but one that costs families a lot when it is not covered.

The excess

The amount you pay yourself on each claim. A low excess on a slightly more expensive policy is often the best calculation.

Paying costs upfront

Some policies pay the hospital directly, others reimburse you afterwards. The difference is huge when you have to pay several thousand Australian dollars upfront.

The sports you do

The line nobody reads. Many basic policies exclude motorbikes, scooters, diving, competitive surfing or high altitude hiking. If you plan to ride a scooter or dive, check it before you sign, not after.

Duration and extension

A working holiday lasts twelve months, sometimes twenty four. Check that the policy covers the real duration and that it can be extended without taking out a new one.

The classic traps

Sub limits. A policy can show a generous overall limit and cap dental at a few hundred euros, or optical at almost nothing.

Work related exclusions. Some leisure policies do not cover accidents that happen during work. On a working holiday, you will work, often in physical jobs.

The initial waiting period. A policy taken out after arrival may cover nothing for several days.

Residence. Some policies require you to live in France when you take them out. So do it before you leave.

Frequently asked questions

Is insurance compulsory for the Australian working holiday visa?

It is not required to get the visa, contrary to what you often read. It is, however, essential in practice, because France is not one of the countries with a reciprocal agreement with the Australian health system. A French citizen therefore has no public cover there.

Does Medicare cover French working holiday makers?

No. Eleven countries have a reciprocal agreement with Medicare, and France is not one of them. Without private insurance, all medical costs are paid by the traveller.

How much does a hospital stay cost in Australia without insurance?

A night in hospital runs into thousands of Australian dollars, and an evacuation from a remote area can exceed several tens of thousands. That is exactly the risk travel insurance covers, and the reason why the medical cost limit is the first criterion to look at.

Can you take out insurance once you are already there?

Some insurers accept it, others do not. Those that do often apply a waiting period during which nothing is covered. Taking it out before you leave is simpler and cheaper.

Are surfing, diving and farm work covered?

Not automatically. Many policies exclude so called risky sports and, more surprisingly, manual work. Yet the 88 days are done precisely on farms or building sites. That is the line to check before you sign, not after.
