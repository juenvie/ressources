Working holiday Australia

The 88 days what really counts.

Industries • Areas • Counting • Payslips • Traps

Three months of specified work unlock a second visa year. But not every job counts, not every region either, and one mistake cancels everything.

In this guide

  1. How it works
  2. Eligible industries
  3. Eligible areas
  4. How to count the days
  5. The evidence to keep
  6. The traps that cancel everything

How it works

To get a second Working Holiday visa, you need to have done three months of specified work during the first one. For a third visa, six months during the second.

Everyone talks about the 88 days, immigration talks about three months. It is the same thing, but the official text is what decides in case of a dispute.

One condition runs through the whole scheme: the work must be paid in line with the law and the industry award. A job paid in cash below the legal minimum does not count, even if you really did it.

The only exception to paid work is volunteering linked to rebuilding after a bushfire or a declared natural disaster.

Eligible industries

The official list is published on the immigration Specified work page, and it is the one that counts.

At the time of writing, it covers plant and animal cultivation in regional areas, fishing and pearling, tree farming and felling, mining, construction, tourism and hospitality in the north and very remote areas, as well as recovery work after a bushfire or a declared natural disaster.

Watch out for a distinction that catches a lot of people out: secondary processing does not count. Picking grapes counts, making wine does not. Raising animals counts, making cured meat does not. Retail does not either.

Immigration also applies a temporary flexibility: work that supports an eligible industry, for example admin or cleaning, may be accepted if it takes place in an eligible postcode. This flexibility can disappear, which is why you should check the official page when you sign.

Eligible areas

The industry is not enough, the place matters just as much. Immigration thinks in postcodes, not town names.

Three categories overlap: regional Australia, northern Australia, and the so called remote and very remote areas. Depending on your industry, a different category applies. Tourism and hospitality only count in the north and very remote areas, whereas agriculture counts across all of regional Australia.

The reliable method fits in one sentence: take the exact postcode of the farm or business, not the nearest town's, and check it on the official page before you accept the job.

How to count the days

The counter follows the days worked, not the days spent there. A part time week is not worth a full week.

The days do not have to be consecutive. You can add them up with several employers, in several regions, at different times of the year.

Piece rate work, paid by weight or by the bin, is converted into days according to precise rules. It is the most misunderstood and most disputed case, so check the official page before accepting that way of being paid.

The evidence to keep

It is up to you to prove your specified work, not your employer. An incomplete file, and the three months do not count.

Keep every payslip, the bank statements showing the salary transfers, your tax file number linked to each employer, and the full contact details of the farms and businesses.

Immigration provides a declaration form for the employer to fill in. Get it signed at the end of each job, not six months later when the farm no longer answers the phone.

The traps that cancel everything

Being paid in cash with no payslip. Very common on farms, and fatal for your application.

Working under an ABN when the job is really employment. Some employers ask for it to avoid contributions. Those days can be challenged, and on top of that you miss out on superannuation.

Taking a job in the right industry but in an ineligible postcode. A few kilometres are enough to cancel everything.

Paying to get a job or a place on a farm. Job scams are a classic in this world, and immigration will not validate those days either.

Trusting a forum rather than the official page. The rules have changed a lot in recent years, and half the advice going around is out of date.
